Hear a sound, describe what changes, and test a way to produce that change in
Vital. You do not need to recover the original preset. Different patches can
produce similar results. Your goal is to make deliberate choices and explain
what you hear before and after each change.

**50 minutes.** Two listening rounds, followed by an application to
[Project 5](/projects/project-5-synth/).

## What you'll practice

By the end of class, you should be able to:

- Describe a sound's loudness shape, brightness, and movement using audible evidence.
- Test whether an amplitude envelope, filter envelope, or LFO produces a chosen feature.
- Compare two versions and explain which change brought you closer to your target.
- Use one of those techniques to improve a patch in your composition.

## Setup and first listen · 5 minutes

1. Open Vital in REAPER with an initialized patch. Keep playback at a comfortable level.
2. Work with a partner if helpful. One person listens and records observations while the other operates Vital; swap roles for Round 2. Each person should make and save a patch.
3. Listen to the first reference twice before touching any controls.
4. Write two observations in everyday language, such as “the note stays loud while the brightness fades.” Add a possible control to test beside each observation.

Start dry, with one oscillator and effects bypassed. Use the same pitch,
register, and note length as the reference as closely as you can. Keep reference
and patch playback at similar perceived loudness so that louder does not win by default.

## Guided comparison · 7 minutes

Watch the instructor compare two versions of a held note:

- An amplitude envelope makes the note fade in loudness.
- A filter envelope makes the note lose brightness while it remains audible.

Predict what will happen before each demonstration. Then describe what changed.
Listen again to the reference and choose which feature you will work on first.
If you are unsure, ask to hear either demonstration again.

## Round 1: Match a change in brightness · 12 minutes

<audio src="../audio/bright-filtered-lead.wav" controls></audio>

**Target:** Recreate the direction and timing of the reference's brightness
change. A recognizable change matters more than an exact timbre match.

1. **Listen and predict · 2 minutes.** Does brightness rise, fall, or stay steady during a note? Does the note's loudness do the same thing? Write a prediction: “If I change ___, I expect to hear ___.”
2. **Build · 5 minutes.** Choose a harmonically rich oscillator, then use a low-pass filter and an envelope to shape brightness. Match the amplitude attack and release well enough to compare the sounds. Save a first attempt.
3. **Test and revise · 3 minutes.** Change one control, such as filter-envelope attack, decay, or modulation amount. Replay the same note. Compare against your saved attempt and record whether your prediction held up. Keep or undo the change based on what you hear.
4. **Partner check · 2 minutes.** Play the reference and your patch. Ask your partner to describe the brightness change without seeing your settings. Agree on one remaining difference and save your patch.

**If you get stuck:** Hold a note, turn the filter cutoff manually, and listen
for the change you want. Then use an envelope to move it automatically. Adjust
one stage at a time. Ask for help connecting the envelope to cutoff if needed.

**Ready for more?** Bypass the filter modulation and explain what disappears.
Then try a different oscillator while preserving the same brightness contour.

## Round 2: Match repeating movement · 13 minutes

<audio src="../audio/lfo-wobble-bass.wav" controls></audio>

**Target:** Recreate the reference's repeating brightness movement on a held
bass note. Leave extra layers, distortion, and effects until the movement works.

1. **Listen and predict · 2 minutes.** Tap along with the changes in brightness. Are they repeating throughout the note or happening only once? Is the movement smooth or abrupt? Predict whether an envelope or LFO would be a useful starting point and explain why.
2. **Build · 6 minutes.** Start a new patch with one oscillator in the bass register. Route it through a low-pass filter and use an LFO to move cutoff. Match the repetition speed first, then the depth of movement and the curve shape. Use tempo sync only if you know the reference tempo; otherwise adjust the rate by ear.
3. **Test and revise · 3 minutes.** Save a first attempt. Predict and test one change to LFO rate, depth, or shape while keeping the other controls steady. Compare the versions and write what changed. If you finish early, bypass modulation to check its contribution.
4. **Partner check · 2 minutes.** Swap listening and operating roles if working together. Ask your partner whether the movement is faster or slower, deeper or shallower than the reference. Make one final adjustment and save.

**If you get stuck:** Slow the LFO down until you can hear one full cycle.
Increase the modulation depth enough to make it obvious, then bring the rate
and depth closer to the reference.

**Ready for more?** Compare a smooth curve with an abrupt curve at the same
rate. Explain which fits the reference better and what you hear that supports it.

## Apply it to Project 5 · 8 minutes

Open one of your own Project 5 patches. Name a musical purpose before editing:
perhaps a bass needs clearer rhythmic movement, or a lead needs a softer entrance.

- Save the original and a revised version.
- Change an envelope or modulation setting using the process you practiced today.
- Listen in your composition, at similar loudness. Decide which version supports the part better and explain why.

Use your own sound-design choices for the project. The reference recreations
are practice; adapting a technique to your own patch is the next step.

## Exit check · 5 minutes

Keep your two practice patches and your Project 5 before-and-after versions.
Write a short note with these three points, then be ready to play one comparison
for the instructor:

1. One audible feature you targeted and the control you chose to test.
2. Your prediction, what actually happened, and why you kept or undid the change.
3. One change you made to your Project 5 patch and how it helps the musical part.

Today's check is formative feedback for Project 5, with no separate graded
submission. Success means you can demonstrate a purposeful change and explain
it with listening evidence. An imperfect match with a clear test is useful work.

## Optional practice after class

Try an [evolving pad](../audio/ambient-pad.wav) or a
[percussive pluck](../audio/percussive-pluck.wav). Pick one audible feature,
predict a control change, save two versions, and compare them before adding
more layers or effects.
