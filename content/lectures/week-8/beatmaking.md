# How drum patterns work

You do not need to read music or play drums to make a convincing beat. A drum
pattern is a short grid of choices: where the kick lands, where the snare or
clap answers it, and how the hi-hat divides the space between them.

Today you will build a two-bar pattern in a browser, rebuild it in REAPER, and
change the second bar so the loop does not sound copied and pasted.

## What you should be able to do

By the end of class, you should be able to:

- find the four main beats in a 16-step grid;
- place a snare or clap on beats 2 and 4 to create a backbeat;
- use hi-hats to divide the beat into smaller parts;
- program a two-bar drum pattern in Sitala; and
- change note placement and velocity to make the second bar different.

## Four useful terms

**Pulse** is the steady beat you might tap with your foot.

**Tempo** is the speed of that pulse, measured in beats per minute, or BPM.

**Backbeat** is a snare or clap on beats 2 and 4. It is common in rock, hip-hop,
R&B, funk, house, and many other styles.

**Subdivision** is how we divide each beat into smaller parts. Hi-hats often
play eighth notes or sixteenth notes and make those divisions audible.

## 1. Feel the layers, 5 minutes

Keep a steady four-count with your foot:

`1 2 3 4 | 1 2 3 4`

Clap only on 2 and 4. Keep the foot going while you clap. Then speak or tap two
even sounds for every beat:

`1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &`

Your foot is holding the pulse, your hands are playing the backbeat, and your
voice is supplying the subdivision. A programmed beat uses the same layers.

## 2. Build the pattern in Ableton Learning Music, 12 minutes

Open [Make Beats](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/make-beats/make-beats) in a
browser. The grid has 16 steps. Steps 1, 5, 9, and 13 mark beats 1, 2, 3, and 4.

Work through these short activities:

1. [Beat and tempo](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/make-beats/beat-and-tempo.html)
2. [Backbeats](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/make-beats/backbeats.html)
3. [Bars](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/make-beats/bars.html)

Then open [Play with beats](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/make-beats/play-with-beats.html)
and make your own one-bar pattern. Start with this recipe if the empty grid is
intimidating:

- Put the kick on beats 1 and 3.
- Put the clap on beats 2 and 4.
- Put the closed hi-hat on every eighth note.
- Add one more kick somewhere between the main beats.

Change the tempo. Notice that the pattern stays the same even though its speed
changes.

## 4. Rebuild your beat in REAPER, 15 minutes

1. Create an instrument track and add Sitala.
2. Create a two-bar MIDI item.
3. Set the MIDI editor grid to sixteenth notes.
4. Add kick notes for the first bar.
5. Add a snare or clap on beats 2 and 4.
6. Add closed hi-hats on eighth notes.
7. Copy the first bar into the second bar.
8. Change at least one kick or hi-hat in the second bar.

If you lose track of the beat, find beats 1, 2, 3, and 4 first. Add the notes
between them only after the main pulse makes sense.

## 5. Make the repetition breathe, 7 minutes

Velocity controls how strongly a MIDI note triggers the drum sampler. Lower
every other hi-hat slightly. Keep the main hats louder and the hats between the
beats quieter.

Try one more change:

- remove a kick from the second bar;
- add a kick just before beat 3 or beat 4;
- replace the last closed hi-hat with an open hi-hat; or
- leave a small gap before the loop returns to bar 1.

Listen before and after each change. Keep the version whose effect you can hear
and describe.

## Exit pattern, 3 minutes

Before you leave, play a two-bar loop for your neighbor or instructor that has:

- kick, snare or clap, and hi-hat;
- a clear pulse and backbeat;
- at least one change in the second bar; and
- at least two different velocity levels.

Save it. This can become the drum track for
[Project 4: Drum Programming and Beatmaking](/projects/project-4-beats/).
