## Objective

Create a 16-32 measure composition from several sampled sources. Program your
own two-bar drum pattern, then use repetition and variation to turn it into a
larger arrangement. Build on Project 1's phrase structure and Project 3's mixing
skills while learning to trigger your own sounds with MIDI.

You may develop the drum pattern and session you started in class. If you reuse
your Project 1 arrangement, replace its original samples with new sources.

## Requirements

1. Create a **16–32 measure composition** using 4- or 8-measure phrases, with a
   clear opening, a change in the middle, and an ending.
2. Include **four sampled instrument tracks**, each triggered by MIDI in
   Speedrum Lite: a drum kit, a chordal hit, a vocal sample, and a found sound.
   Effects returns and one optional audio-loop track are additional tracks.
3. Program an **original two-bar drum pattern** using 99Sounds: 99 Drum Samples I.
   Include a kick, snare or clap, and hi-hat or percussion; at least one change
   in bar 2; and more than one velocity level on at least one drum part.
4. Make **at least one purposeful sample transformation** beyond trimming or
   level adjustment: stretch, reverse, pitch-shift, or chop and reorder a sample.
5. Balance the parts with faders and panning so each has a clear role, with
   **no clipping** on the tracks or master. Use EQ or compression where useful.
6. Create **at least one shared reverb or delay return** receiving sends from
   two or more instrument tracks.
7. Submit a **rendered WAV, a complete zipped project with all kits and samples,
   and source and decision notes** as described in the submission instructions.

## Build the arrangement

Develop your two-bar drum pattern into **16–32 measures**, using 4- or 8-measure
phrases as in Project 1. Include a clear opening, a change in the middle, and
an ending. Bring parts in and out, leave space for the vocal or chordal hit,
and make at least one phrase differ from the previous one. A simple arrangement
with audible choices can meet the full requirements.

## Sample categories

### Programmed drum pattern

Load at least three one-shot samples into one Speedrum Lite instance: a kick, a snare
or clap, and a hi-hat or another percussion sound. Program an original two-bar
pattern. The second bar must contain at least one change, and at least one drum
part must use more than one velocity level.

Use drum one-shots from **99Sounds: 99 Drum Samples I**, the pack used in our
[MIDI and Speedrum Lite lab](/lectures/week-7/midi-intro/#prepare-three-drum-sounds).
Choose your own combination from the pack or develop the pattern you started
in class. Include the pack name and filenames in your source notes. You may
layer a sampled break underneath your programmed drums, but the break does
not replace the programmed pattern.

Example: [Most-used drum breaks on
WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/song-tag/Drum%20Breaks%20%28Most%20Used%29/)

### Chordal hits

Find a sample or loop of a chordal instrument, such as piano or guitar. Trim a
short chord or phrase and trigger it with MIDI. You do not need to play it
chromatically across the keyboard.

Examples: [Piano samples on
WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/song-tag/Piano/) and [Johannes Brahms
samples on WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Johannes-Brahms/)

### Vocal sample

Use a short vocal phrase or hit as a melodic or rhythmic element.

Examples:

- [Mariah Carey samples on WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Mariah-Carey/)
- [Lyn Collins, *Think (About It)*, on WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Lyn-Collins/Think-\(About-It\)/)
- [Burial's *Archangel* sampling Ray J's *One Wish* on WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/2631/Burial-Archangel-Ray-J-One-Wish/)

### Found sounds

Choose a sample from [Freesound.org](http://freesound.org) to add texture or "ear
candy." You could use vinyl noise, a field recording, or another short sound.
You may instead record your own found sound with a Zoom recorder or your phone.

> In addition to the four MIDI-triggered tracks, you may use one audio loop.

## Setup and sample preparation

Install the free **Speedrum Lite** plug-in and follow the
[download, installation, and sample-saving instructions](/lectures/week-7/midi-intro/).
For routing help, review [faders, panning, and sends](/lectures/week-6/rough-balance/).
Keep EQ and compression on individual tracks as inserts when you use them.

Capture or download your source audio, then trim and render the section you
want to load into Speedrum Lite. Already-trimmed one-shots can be loaded
directly. Follow the
[worked vocal-sampling example](/lectures/week-7/midi-intro/#worked-example-turn-a-vocal-recording-into-a-midi-part)
for extracting, transforming, and sequencing a phrase. Read the sampling and
copyright note below before choosing your sources.

You may use a YouTube downloader or record system audio with Audacity's WASAPI
loopback on Windows ([setup guide](https://support.audacityteam.org/basics/recording-desktop-audio)).
If you need to isolate a vocal or instrument, [Ultimate Vocal
Remover](https://github.com/Anjok07/ultimatevocalremovergui) is an optional tool.
It runs locally and separates a mix into stems, though results depend on the
source. Audition the stems before using them and list the tool in your source
notes. See the AI tools section of the [syllabus](/syllabus/).

## Sampling and copyright

For this project, you may sample from existing recordings, including audio
captured from online videos. That is workable here because the project is
unpublished coursework. Releasing music made from samples requires clearance:
permission to use both the recording (the master) and the song (the composition).
Those rights usually belong to different people. The Winstons, whose *"Amen,
Brother"* break underpins entire genres, famously never received royalties.

Do not upload Project 4 to SoundCloud, Spotify, or YouTube. Submit it only to D2L
and share it in class. For a version you can release, use cleared sources such as
[Freesound](https://freesound.org), your own recordings, public-domain
compositions such as Brahms works, or a paid clearance service such as
[Tracklib](https://www.tracklib.com/). Check the license for every Freesound file;
some require attribution. We will discuss the rights behind famous samples during
the sampling history lesson.

## Submission instructions

Submit to D2L the following:

- A compressed folder with the complete project, all audio files, and all samples.
  Save each Speedrum Lite kit with its samples inside the project folder and
  include those kit files and sample subfolders. Reopen a copy of the complete
  folder and check every sampled track before submitting.
- A rendered WAV file of the final master mix.
- A short text document with:
  - Every sample's source and filename, including the 99Sounds pack name and
    any required license credits. For your own recordings, state what you recorded.
  - Any source-separation tool used, if applicable.
  - The sample you transformed, what you changed, and its audible purpose.
  - One mixing decision: the problem you heard and how balance, panning,
    processing, or the shared effects send helped.
