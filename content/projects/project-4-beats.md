## Objective

Create a 16-32 measure composition from several sampled sources. Use each sample
category below on its own track, with one instance of Sitala per track. You may
rework your loop project, but you must use new samples.

## Requirements

- Include at least four tracks. Each track must use MIDI to trigger Sitala.
- Capture audio from your source recordings. You may use a YouTube downloader or
  record system audio with Audacity's WASAPI loopback on Windows ([setup
  guide](https://support.audacityteam.org/basics/recording-desktop-audio)). Read
  the sampling and copyright note below before choosing your sources.
- Select and render the part of each recording you want to sample, then load it
  into Sitala.
- If you need to isolate part of a recording, such as drums without bass or a
  vocal without the accompaniment, you may use [Ultimate Vocal
  Remover](https://github.com/Anjok07/ultimatevocalremovergui). It runs locally
  and separates a mix into stems, though the results depend on the source.
  Audition the stems before using them. List the tool in your submission notes
  along with your sample sources. See the AI tools section of the
  [syllabus](/syllabus/).
- Use the production techniques covered in class, including EQ, compression,
  reverb, and delay.
- Create at least one reverb or delay send, as you did in Project 3. Route two or
  more tracks to it so they share the same space. Keep EQ and compression on
  individual tracks as inserts. Review the [rough balance
  lesson](/lectures/week-6/rough-balance/) if you need help with routing.
- You may manipulate the samples beyond basic EQ and compression by stretching,
  reversing, pitch-shifting, or processing them with other effects.
- You may work in a new genre or combine genres, but this is optional.

## Sample categories

### Drum loop

Use a drum loop from a classic breakbeat, such as *"Amen, Brother"* by The
Winstons, as the rhythmic foundation of your composition.

Example: [Most-used drum breaks on
WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/song-tag/Drum%20Breaks%20%28Most%20Used%29/)

### Chordal hits

Find a sample or loop of a chordal instrument, such as piano or guitar.

Examples: [Piano samples on
WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/song-tag/Piano/) and [Johannes Brahms
samples on WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Johannes-Brahms/)

### Vocal sample

Use a short vocal phrase or hit as a melodic or rhythmic element.

Examples:

- [Mariah Carey samples on WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Mariah-Carey/)
- [Lyn Collins, *Think (About It)*, on WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/Lyn-Collins/Think-\(About-It\)/)
- [Burial's *Archangel* sampling Ray J's *One Wish* on WhoSampled](https://www.whosampled.com/sample/2631/Burial-Archangel-Ray-J-One-Wish/)

### Found sounds

Choose a sample from [Freesound.org](http://freesound.org) to add texture or "ear
candy." You could use vinyl noise, a field recording, or another short sound.
You may instead record your own found sound with a Zoom recorder or your phone.

> In addition to the four MIDI-triggered tracks, you may use one audio loop.

## Sampling and copyright

For this project, you may sample from existing recordings, including audio
captured from online videos. That is workable here because the project is
unpublished coursework. Releasing music made from samples requires clearance:
permission to use both the recording (the master) and the song (the composition).
Those rights usually belong to different people. The Winstons, whose *"Amen,
Brother"* break underpins entire genres, famously never received royalties.

Do not upload Project 4 to SoundCloud, Spotify, or YouTube. Submit it only to D2L
and share it in class. For a version you can release, use cleared sources such as
[Freesound](https://freesound.org), your own recordings, public-domain
compositions such as Brahms works, or a paid clearance service such as
[Tracklib](https://www.tracklib.com/). Check the license for every Freesound file;
some require attribution. We will discuss the rights behind famous samples during
the WhoSampled class session.
